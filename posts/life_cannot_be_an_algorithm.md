# Life Cannot Be an Algorithm

I believe it is a core feature of [software brain](https://www.theverge.com/podcast/917029/software-brain-ai-backlash-databases-automation) to believe that everything can — and should — be optimized.
That each aspect of life can be broken down into an algorithm which can be optimized.

For example, I have spent many hours of my life reading books about software engineering best practices.
If you have not read any of these books, you can imagine them to be self help books, written for software engineers.
They have been incredibly informative, and I would recommend any one of them to anyone getting started in their careers.
I do not regret reading them.
But, as they are all written for a technical audience, they tend to architect themselves in a specific way.

Everyone of these books starts with an opening statement. Something like:

> "Software engineering is hard, but we can make it easier by pointing our engineering prowess back upon ourselves. By changing *how* you work, you can improve your effectiveness. By improving your effectiveness, you can produce better, more successful products. By adopting these concrete series of steps, you can make better decisions and move faster towards building better software."

Does that sound familiar?
Here's the definition of algorithm, according to Merriam Webster's Dictionary:

> Algorithm (noun): a specific step-by-step procedure for solving a problem or accomplishing some end

These software engineering books work on the implicit assumption that the work itself must be done by an algorithm.
I will not say that this view is objectively wrong, but it does make me a little sad.
my working life must exist to serve and improve upon an algorithm
