# The Best of Om Malik

I had never heard of Om Malik before he died.

In the time since, I've perused his extensive catalog of blog posts and articles.
No description of his writing could be more accurate than just one word: engrossing.
Indeed, I could barely stop myself from spending all my time reading.

In the (admittedly Westernized) version of Buddhism in which I was raised, [Om](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Om) is the sound that contains the entire universe.
According to my parents, the universe did not start with a bang, but with a slow and progressively loudening guttural hum. An "Om".

It's possible I am biased and my brain was simply doing a kind of word association.
Although they appear to merely be words on a page, I often feel that Om's work almost always carries a rare introspective insight.
Perhaps it's truth. Perhaps it's something else.

In any case, I wanted to dedicate my blog post today to Om and some of the writing that I find to be some of his best work.
I do not claim to be an Om scholar, so I would be shocked if these truly constitute his "best" work.
Instead, I'd like you to view this as a list of some my favorite pieces that I have read in the short time since his unfortunate passing.

## [We Are Living in Pinocchio's World](https://om.co/2026/05/25/we-are-living-in-pinocchios-world/)

I'm not sure the lesson contained in this blog post could be written down in any form other than with the precise words Om used to deliver it, so I won't try.
Here's a taste of the core lesson I took away.

The tech industry has evolved from a place run by institutions who identified themselves [as opposed to corporate greed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don%27t_be_evil) to a place populated by profit seeking bandits.
There are some who seek to fight the good fight (like the wonderful people at Automattic), but most simply don't care.
Many justify immoral behavior by claiming it's for the greater good.

The hard part, Om says, is that it's become difficult to filter out the people who care from the people that don't.
In many ways, we've invented ourselves into the very world Pinocchio's story warns us about.

> "Pinocchio is a story about a society organized around deception." 
> 
> <div align="right"> — Om Malik </div>

It often feels like that's the society we live in today.

## [A Final Goodbye](https://om.co/2022/08/08/goodbye-vijay-gill-dear-friend/)

In reading both this article and the numerous similar articles that have cropped up after his death, I am reminded that we in the world of technology are often myopic in our attention to technology.
We focus on the most recent advancements and neglect to pay attention to the people behind them.
Even when we do pay attention to the humans behind the curtain, we still so rarely view them as any more than subjects of admiration.

When we build products, too, we forget about the people we do it with.
It's all lost to the sea of Jira tickets and Codex sessions.

I think Om understood this better than most, and he strove to do better.

## [Social Internet Is Dead. Get Over It.](https://om.co/2023/10/15/social-internet-is-dead-get-used-to-it/)

In a similar spirit to the first item in my list today, this article paints a picture of the "social" internet as it is today. 

Om claims that the evolution from the healthy social internet of old to the one we have today begun "to atrophy from the moment we started treating them as our personal marketing channels."

This idea closely mirrors something I've heard time and time again from [Jason Adams](https://jason.blog/):
A well run business doesn't need marketing; it needs a mission that unites itself with its customers.
Om describes an early internet (that I wasn't old enough to witness at the time) that did exactly that.
Early internet companies had bold visions to improve the lives of everyone on Earth.
Their customers responded to that well, and were happy to help in any way possible.

Today's internet companies feel corporate.
Their only motive: Seeking shareholder value.

There's nothing inherently wrong with that, but it has created an internet that feels, well, like it's been organized around deception.
